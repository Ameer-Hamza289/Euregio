History

History of the Rhine-Waal Euroregion

The Second World War had left deep scars in the region around Kleve, Emmerich, Arnhem and Nijmegen. Initially private contacts gradually led to cross-border exchange at an official level.

1963

First joint conference of the municipalities of Kleve, Emmerich, Arnhem and Nijmegen on the theme of cross-border perspectives through the construction of 'Rijksweg 15' (the current A3/A12). More conferences followed.

In the meantime, the Rhine-Waal Euregion has developed from a loose organisation for cross-border cooperation between municipalities mainly located near the border into a public body with approximately 55 members. In the 1970s, the working area extended to 1,600 km² with a total of 750,000 inhabitants, but today it covers an area of ​​8,663 km² with a population of approximately 4.2 million.
